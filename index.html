How Far Can You Ramp?

906.4K visits · Solo design and implementation

Designed a repeatable driving-simulator economy: drive, launch, earn currency based on landing distance, and purchase Ramp Speed upgrades to travel farther.

Technical breakdown · Visual scripting

Progression pipeline: A landing-distance result determines currency earnings, which feed back into Ramp Speed upgrades and longer runs.

Rebirth and persistence: At the maximum speed tier, players can reset spendable money and upgrades for a permanent +1 landing-earnings multiplier while retaining lifetime statistics.

Randomized rewards: Golden Eggs award randomized Gold amounts across rarity tiers; rarity colors, sound and reward values communicate the outcome.

Economy tuning: Researched simulator pricing, adjusted upgrade costs and reward odds, and tuned how an internal speed variable mapped to the player-facing speed display.

Bug investigation: After observing vehicles disrupting players at spawn, added a restricted-area respawn trigger and an explanation. No measured before/after incident rate is available.

Rec Room · Unity · Visual scripting · Two-person team

Big Hands

840.6K visits · Gameplay systems and interaction design

Designed and implemented asymmetric multiplayer interactions for VR players with giant hands and screen players who can escape, hide and use the environment.

Technical breakdown · Visual scripting

Asymmetric player roles: VR players grab and carry screen players. Screen players can escape, use cars, hide, or unlock keys that prevent being picked up.

Interactive experiences: Implemented gameplay around planet journeys, a freezer that turns players to ice, a timed microwave and a cannon that launches players toward a target.

Player lifecycle: Screen players respawn after experiences so they can rejoin the interaction loop.

Usability fix: Originally, releasing a giant hand caused it to respawn immediately. After player feedback, changed the behavior to delay respawn so VR players could keep using the hand more conveniently.

Player-facing effects: The nuke combined visual effects and audio and became the game's best-selling item.

Team contribution: I designed and implemented interaction and gameplay logic; my teammate sourced and placed environment assets.

Unity · iOS · Published game

Sticker Puzzles: Match Scenes

Published on the App Store

Built and released a mobile puzzle game centered on selecting stickers, placing them in scenes, and completing matching challenges.

  • Implemented sticker selection, target matching, puzzle completion, and optional hints in Unity.
  • Found a usability issue through playtesting: placing stickers precisely could feel frustrating.
  • Improved the interaction by snapping stickers into the correct positions.

Technical breakdown

Interaction flow: Sticker selection and placement, target matching, puzzle completion and optional hint behavior built in Unity.

Usability issue: Playtesting showed that requiring precise manual placement made the interaction frustrating.

Iteration: Revised placement so stickers snap into the correct positions, making the intended action easier to complete.

QA focus: Test placement near the target from multiple release positions and confirm expected snapping behavior. The video predates this improvement.
